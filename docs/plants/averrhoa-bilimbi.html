The plant today

Averrhoa bilimbi (commonly known as bilimbi, orkkaappuli, cucumber tree, or tree sorrel) is a fruit-bearing tree in the family Oxalidaceae. It is native to the Maluku Islands and Sulawesi in Indonesia, and has become naturalised and is common throughout Southeast Asia. It is cultivated in parts of tropical South Asia and the Americas. It bears edible but extremely sour fruit. It is a close relative of the carambola tree.

Sa Tagalog

Ang kamias, kamyas o kalamyas (Ingles: camias ginger lily) ay isang maliit na punong tropikal na nagbubunga ng mga maaasim na prutas na maaaring kainin. Tinatawag ding kamias ang bunga ng punong ito. Ito ay may taas na 15-30 talampakan at may luntian na mga bunga na nagtataglay sa kanyang katas nang asidong oxalic.

What the 1901 book says

A historical record, quoted as printed. Doses and remedies here are not medical advice.

Uses

The small fruit of the camia springing from the branches and trunks of the trees is widely known in the Philippines, where they eat it green, pickled, and in salad; and when ripe fresh and preserved. Its qualities and therapeutic applications are the same as those of the following species.

Botanical description

A tree 4–5 meters high with odd-pinnate leaves. Leaflets 12 pairs, ovate, linear, acute, soft and downy. Flowers small, pinkish or purplish, on trunk and branches. Stamens 10, five alternately longer. Pistils divergent. Fruit oblong, obtuse at the end, with five broad ribs.

What the 1985 book uses it for

A record of home remedies from 1985, with the book's amounts and doses reworded. It is not medical advice.

  • FeverLeaf decoction for a sponge bath.Boil 3 cups of chopped fresh leaves in a gallon of water for 15 minutes and strain.Page 25
  • HemorrhoidsLeaf decoction for a sitz bath.Boil 3 cups of chopped fresh leaves in 2 gallons of water for 10 minutes and strain. Sitz bath twice a day.Page 28
  • MumpsMashed fruit as a poultice on the swollen glands.Leave on for 30 minutes, three times a day.Page 33

The caption gives the scientific and English names of kamia, the white ginger lily (Hedychium coronarium). The local names listed, and the fruit the remedies call for, belong to kamias, Averrhoa bilimbi, so the entry is filed there.
